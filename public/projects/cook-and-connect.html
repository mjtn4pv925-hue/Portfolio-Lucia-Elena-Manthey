Cook & Connect

Team cooking events that turn integration into a shared table.

Context
Design Thinking for Social Innovation, Neu-Ulm University of Applied Sciences, 2024
My role
Stakeholder outreach, solution design, event portfolio design
Methods
User interviews, design sprint, customer journeys, value proposition canvas, prototyping, user testing

The challenge

Refugee women in Germany face a double barrier. Language courses, childcare and unrecognised qualifications make it hard to find work. And many rarely meet locals, which makes integration even harder. Migrant mothers in Germany have an employment rate 29 percentage points lower than mothers without a migration background.

At the same time, companies are looking for team events that mean something. Many want to engage with refugees but don't know how to create real, everyday contact.

Our idea – a win-win around one table

Cook & Connect brings both sides together: corporate team cooking events where refugee women lead the kitchen as chefs. Together, teams cook a three-course menu from the women's home countries, such as Afghanistan, Eritrea, Syria and Ukraine.

For refugee women

social and economic impact

  • Flexible, fairly paid work that fits around childcare and needs no formal qualifications
  • Real contact with locals and everyday language practice
  • A stage for their culture: their recipes, their stories, their pride

For companies

team spirit with purpose

  • A relaxed, hands-on team event that strengthens collaboration
  • Intercultural exchange in an informal setting, where conversations flow naturally
  • A visible, tangible contribution to their social responsibility

What we did

  • Listened first. We reached out to Flüchtlingsrat Ulm, Caritas and the Asylcafé Ulm and interviewed four refugee women from Ukraine and Eritrea, plus an Asylcafé staff member.
  • Mapped both sides. In a three-day design sprint we wrote How-Might-We questions for the women and for companies, and mapped one customer journey for each.
  • Built three prototypes. A website for companies, a flyer for the women, and an event portfolio with the event flow, menus, location options and pricing.
  • Tested with both target groups. We showed the prototypes to two refugee women and two corporate employees, one of whom books team events for her company.

What we learned

  1. Our biggest fear was wrong.

    We worried the women would hesitate to cook with company teams. Instead, they were eager to take part and wanted to tell their friends.

  2. Both sides loved the exchange.

    The women valued sharing recipes from home. The company side said the format creates a relaxed atmosphere where people naturally learn about each other's cultures.

  3. Right message, wrong channel.

    Flyers didn't work for the women. They find offers on social media, so that is where outreach has to happen.

  4. Show everything upfront.

    Companies wanted all details, prices and the chefs' stories right away. Their typical budget turned out to be around €50 per person, so we planned tiered pricing.

The event portfolio

I designed the event portfolio that introduces companies to the concept: the event flow, three menu options, location options and transparent pricing.

What came next

Cook & Connect stayed a concept, but the idea stayed with me: bringing people together over food, stories and culture. It became the starting point for my own project, cambiando platos.
